The industry has fallen out of love with removing friction. Every few weeks there’s another piece arguing that when we stripped the effort out of experiences, we stripped out the meaning too. Daniel Kahneman’s research explains why. We don’t remember experiences as a whole. We remember the most intense moment and how it ended, and frictionless design has spent years smoothing out both. It’s a point we’ve been making for the best part of a decade, so I’m glad of the company. But it raises the obvious next question. Then what?

More friction isn’t automatically the answer. Give everyone permission to add a memorable moment and you get a dozen different ideas, each pulling in its own direction. At best the experience becomes inconsistent. At worst, incoherent.

So what fills the gap? Chip and Dan Heath write about creating peaks in The Power of Moments. The peaks that matter here are the ones led by the brand. A moment that stands out should elevate the brand and make what it stands for more obvious, wherever it happens. On a screen, at a checkout, on the phone, in the packaging something arrives in. A digital experience is part of the brand experience, but not every brand experience is digital.

That means deciding, deliberately, where a brand gives people ease and where it asks something of them. The tools we use to remove friction are the same ones we use to put the brand back in at the points that matter most. Most brands have guidelines for how they look and sound. Very few have a guide for what slowing down should look like, and why.

To pick up another favourite LinkedIn topic, this gets sharper as AI agents absorb the transactional parts of the journey. What people still experience directly will increasingly be the parts that carry meaning. Someone has to design those on purpose.

The best time to do that is when a brand is being created, transformed or evolved. Not afterwards, bolted on as a CX fix.

The question I’d put to any brand team right now: where should your brand ask something more from someone, and what do they get back?
